1. Advanced Contrastive Decoding (Probability Manipulation)

These methods work by comparing the "normal" model against a "flawed" version to highlight and subtract likely hallucinations.

    Multi-Model Contrastive Decoding (MCD): Uses three models simultaneously: a base model, an "evil" (hallucination-prone) model, and a "truthful" model. By looking for tokens that the evil model likes but the truthful model also identifies, it prunes "hallucinatory branches" in real-time.

    Regularized Contrastive Decoding (RCD): A more efficient single-model approach. It uses Adversarial Fine-Tuning to mathematically synthesize "worst-case scenario" hallucinations. During inference, it penalizes any tokens that match these synthesized patterns.

2. Layer-Wise Interventions (Mechanistic Exploitation)

Research discovered a "Depth-Factual Dichotomy": shallow layers often hold the raw truth, while deeper layers get "distracted" by linguistic patterns and start hallucinating.

    DoLa & LayerCD: These algorithms contrast the output of early layers against the final layers. If the final layer deviates too sharply from the early "factual" layers, the system corrects the trajectory.

    Dynamic Correction (DeCo): Specifically for Multimodal models (images + text). It found that layers 20–28 usually have the correct visual data before the deep text layers "override" it with a hallucination. DeCo mathematically reintegrates that "lost" visual knowledge into the final result.

3. Representation Engineering (Latent Space Steering)

Instead of looking at the final word probabilities, these techniques reach into the "hidden states" (the geometric math inside the AI) to force them into a "truthful" shape.

    Truthfulness Separator Vectors (TSV): A "plug-and-play" method that adds a tiny steering vector (only 0.00005% of the model's parameters) to the internal math. This pushes the AI's internal representations away from "hallucination clusters" and toward "truth clusters."

    Dynamic Multimodal Activation Steering (DMAS): A database-driven approach for Vision-Language models. It identifies specific "truthfulness heads" and "perception heads" in the transformer and boosts their signals based on the semantic context of the image.

The "vLLM" Era (2026)

The industry has moved these theories into production through tools like HaluGate. This system uses a "Sentinel" to flag risky prompts and an "NLI Explanation" layer to verify tokens in C++/CUDA. By combining this with Speculative Decoding (using a small model to "guess" and a big model to "verify"), 2026-era systems can mitigate hallucinations without slowing down the user experience.

    Key Takeaway: The "hallucination problem" is being solved not by more data, but by treating the LLM as a causal machine that can be steered geometrically and mechanically in real-time.
