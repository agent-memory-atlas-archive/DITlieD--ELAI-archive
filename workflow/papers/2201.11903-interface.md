Algorithm: Chain-of-Thought Prompting (CoT)
ArXiv: 2201.11903
Paper: Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models
Authors: Wei, Wang, Schuurmans, Bosma, Ichter, Xia, Chi, Le, Zhou (Google Brain, 2022)

WHAT IT IS:
CoT augments few-shot exemplars by including intermediate natural-language reasoning steps between the problem and the final answer, forming (input, chain-of-thought, output) triples. The model learns from these demonstrations to generate its own reasoning trace before producing the answer. The technique is purely prompting-based (no fine-tuning) and was shown to elicit emergent reasoning capability in sufficiently large models. Whether models actually "reason" vs. pattern-match remains an open question per the authors.

IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS:
Prompt format: concatenate k exemplars, each structured as [problem text] [reasoning steps in natural language] [final answer]. Reasoning steps must precede the answer (ablation shows reversed order hurts). Standard k=8 exemplars; AQuA used k=4 (multiple-choice format). Decoding: greedy decoding (temperature=0, argmax). Optional extension: pipe arithmetic sub-expressions through Python eval() as an external calculator to eliminate arithmetic errors. Self-consistency (majority vote over N sampled generations) referenced as a follow-up improvement but not the baseline method. No fine-tuning; exemplars are hand-written.

Exact few-shot template (from paper Figure 1):
  Q: [math/reasoning problem]
  A: [step 1 reasoning]. [step 2 reasoning]. ... The answer is [X].
  [repeat for k exemplars, then add the test question]

STALENESS VERDICT: PARTIALLY-STALE
The original mechanism (few-shot CoT with hand-written exemplars) is largely absorbed into instruction-tuning and RLHF pipelines by 2025. Modern instruction-tuned models like Qwen2.5-Coder-7B already exhibit CoT-like reasoning behavior without explicit few-shot exemplars. However, explicit CoT prompting still improves performance measurably on hard reasoning tasks for smaller models; 2025 research (arXiv:2506.07712 "Through the Valley: Path to Effective Long CoT Training for Small Language Models") shows CoT SFT+RL still outperforms baseline instruction tuning for sub-10B models. The specific "PaLM 540B baseline" numbers are obsolete. The structural insight (show reasoning before answer) survives as a design principle.
Cite: arXiv:2506.07712 (2025) — explicit CoT training still yields gains for small LMs.

RELEVANCE TO REASONING-STATE ENGINE:
- Cross-boundary state: CoT externalizes intermediate reasoning into text, making it portable across context boundaries; a reasoning-state engine can store/restore the chain as plain text and resume mid-chain — directly applicable.
- Candidate/confidence system: CoT traces expose intermediate conclusions that can be independently verified or scored; each step is a candidate assertion amenable to a confidence gate before the next step is committed.
- When more reasoning helps vs hurts: paper shows CoT hurts on small models (<100B in 2022 terms, roughly <7B today) and has diminishing returns on simple tasks; the engine should detect task complexity and skip chain generation for low-complexity inputs.

Inputs:
prompt_exemplars | list[str] | k=8 hand-written (input, reasoning, answer) triples | mandatory; must precede final question in context
test_input | str | the new problem to solve | mandatory
decoding_mode | enum | greedy (default) or sampled (for self-consistency) | optional, default greedy

Outputs:
reasoning_chain | str | free-form natural language | must precede the answer string in model output
final_answer | str | extracted from end of generation | invariant: appears after the reasoning chain

Hyperparameters:
k_exemplars | 8 (4 for AQuA) | 4-16 | low sensitivity shown in ablation (Fig 11); diminishing returns past 8
model_size | >=100B recommended (2022); >=7B modern | N/A | hard threshold: CoT hurts below emergence threshold
temperature | 0 (greedy) | 0 for greedy, >0 for self-consistency | self-consistency uses >0 then majority vote

Critical invariants (testable assertions):
assert reasoning_steps_precede_answer(output), "chain must come before final answer"
assert len(exemplars) >= 4, "too few exemplars degrades CoT, paper uses 8"
assert model_size_sufficient_for_emergence(model), "CoT harms sub-threshold models"
assert 'The answer is' in output or answer_marker_present(output), "final answer must be delimited"

Complexity:
Time: O(k * avg_chain_length) tokens added to prompt; chain generation adds ~2-5x tokens over standard prompting
Space: prompt length grows linearly with k and chain length; at k=8, expect 300-600 extra tokens in context
Parallelism: none inherent; self-consistency variant parallelizes N independent samples

Failure modes:
CoT degrades performance on models below the emergence threshold (roughly <100B params in 2022, lower bar today)
Incorrect reasoning chain can still produce the correct answer (unfaithful trace)
Minor arithmetic errors accumulate through chains (46% of failures had "almost correct" reasoning)
Major semantic misunderstanding in 54% of remaining failures; chain cannot fix a confused premise
No guarantee of factual correctness in any generated step
Performance drops on symbolic tasks with longer input sequences (length generalization fails OOD)

Implementation pitfalls (do NOT do this):
Do NOT place the final answer before the reasoning chain in exemplars
Do NOT use CoT on tasks trivially solved by standard prompting (adds tokens, no benefit)
Do NOT assume a green CoT trace means correct reasoning (trace can be post-hoc rationalization)
Do NOT use greedy decoding for self-consistency (requires sampling with temperature > 0)
Do NOT hand-write exemplars from a different domain than the test distribution
